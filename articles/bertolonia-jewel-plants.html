Bertolonia: The Jewel Plants of the Forest Floor

EM
Elena Marsh, Botanical Writer
•July 8, 2026•6 min read

Most of the genera covered on this site are grown for their flowers. Bertolonia is the exception — a small genus of around 14 species, native to the tropical forest floor of Brazil, grown almost entirely for its leaves. The common name, "jewel plant," undersells it slightly: velvety, iridescent foliage in combinations of silver, pink, bronze, and deep carmine, patterned in a way that looks closer to hand-painted silk than typical plant tissue.

A genus built for shade

Bertolonia species grow low and creeping, rarely exceeding 15cm in height, spreading along the humid forest floor beneath a closed canopy. That native habitat explains almost everything about how to keep one alive: high humidity, warm and stable temperatures, and light shade rather than any direct sun, which scorches the delicate leaf surface quickly.

Why the leaves look the way they do

The shimmering quality often seen in Bertolonia foliage comes from a structural effect — microscopic air layers just beneath the leaf's surface scatter light in a way that produces an iridescent sheen, rather than the color coming from pigment alone. It's the same general category of effect seen in a handful of other shade-forest plants prized for foliage, and it's part of why Bertolonia has a devoted following among terrarium and vivarium builders specifically, where the humidity stays consistently high enough for the plant to actually thrive.

Growing Bertolonia

  • Humidity: consistently high — this genus struggles badly in average home humidity and is best suited to a closed terrarium or humidity dome rather than an open windowsill.
  • Light: bright shade, never direct sun.
  • Soil: light, well-draining, kept evenly moist rather than allowed to dry out fully between waterings.
Bertolonia is a reminder that "showy" in Melastomataceae doesn't always mean flowers — sometimes the family's real spectacle is happening in the leaf tissue itself.
