Overwatering vs. Underwatering: Telling Them Apart in Melastomes

PN
Priya Nair, Field Botanist
•July 10, 2026•6 min read

A surprising number of melastome problems that look identical at first glance — yellowing leaves, wilting, leaf drop — trace back to opposite causes. Overwatering and underwatering can produce confusingly similar-looking symptoms, and treating one as if it were the other tends to make things considerably worse rather than better.

The symptoms that overlap

Both overwatered and underwatered melastomes commonly show: yellowing leaves, a wilted or drooping appearance, and leaf drop. Reacting to wilting by adding more water — the intuitive response — is exactly the wrong move if the actual problem is waterlogged roots rather than dry ones, and it's a genuinely common way people accidentally kill an otherwise recoverable plant.

How to actually tell the difference

  • Check the soil, not just the leaves. Underwatered soil is dry throughout, often pulling away from the pot's edges. Overwatered soil stays visibly wet, sometimes with a sour or musty smell — a sign of root rot beginning.
  • Look at which leaves are affected first. Underwatering typically shows up as crisping, dry leaf edges starting with older growth. Overwatering more often produces soft, mushy yellowing, sometimes starting at the base of the plant.
  • Consider the pot itself. A pot without drainage holes, or a decorative outer pot trapping water at the base, is a strong overwatering risk factor regardless of how carefully you're watering.
When in doubt, check the soil with a finger before doing anything else — it settles the question faster than staring at the leaves ever will.

Recovery steps

For overwatering: stop watering immediately, improve drainage, and in serious cases, remove the plant from its pot to inspect roots for rot, trimming away any that are soft, dark, or foul-smelling. For underwatering: water thoroughly and consistently rather than in small, frequent amounts, which often fails to fully rehydrate the root ball.
