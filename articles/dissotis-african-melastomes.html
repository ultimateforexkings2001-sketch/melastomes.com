Dissotis: Africa's Showiest Melastome Genus

DK
Daniel Kwan, Horticulture Writer
•July 8, 2026•6 min read

While Melastomataceae skews heavily toward the Americas — roughly two-thirds of the family's genera are New World natives — Dissotis is one of the more prominent exceptions. This genus of around 140 species is native across tropical and subtropical Africa, where it ranges from sprawling groundcovers to upright shrubs, nearly all sharing the same striking trait: large, vivid purple to magenta flowers that stand out clearly against surrounding vegetation.

A genus with a naming complication

Dissotis is a good example of how taxonomy in this family stays genuinely unsettled. One widely grown species, commonly sold as a groundcover under the name Dissotis rotundifolia, is also frequently classified as Heterotis rotundifolia — the same plant, two different accepted names depending on which taxonomic treatment is being followed. This isn't a mistake on anyone's part; it reflects an ongoing, legitimate disagreement about how closely related the two genera actually are.

Where it grows and how it's used

In its native range, Dissotis species are common along roadsides, wetland margins, and disturbed ground, tolerating a wide range of soil conditions. Several species have found their way into cultivation elsewhere as ground covers or border plants, prized specifically for how long and reliably they flower through the growing season.

Few genera in this family put on as much color, for as long, with as little fuss as Dissotis.

Growing notes

Dissotis generally tolerates more sun than many of its rainforest-understory relatives, reflecting its more open, disturbed-ground native habitat. Regular moisture and a reasonably rich soil keep it flowering well; like most of the family, it responds poorly to prolonged drought.

The Dissotis/Heterotis naming overlap is a genuinely useful entry point into how species boundaries get decided — see our piece on reading a botanical name for more on how synonyms like this arise.
