Bellucia: The Amazon's Fruit-Bearing Melastome Trees

M
Melastomes.com Editorial
•October 7, 2026•4 min read

Bellucia is a genus of small to medium trees from the lowland and foothill forests of tropical America. It stands out within the melastome family for its large flowers and its unusually large, fleshy fruits.

Quick facts. Family: Melastomataceae. Range: tropical Central and South America, especially the Amazon basin. Habitat: forest gaps, clearings and secondary growth. Growth form: small to medium trees. Fruit: large, fleshy berries with many small seeds.

What sets Bellucia apart

Many melastome fruits are tiny berries or dry capsules. Bellucia produces fruit several centimetres across, with a soft, sweet-sour pulp full of small seeds. In parts of Brazil one species is known as goiaba-de-anta, which translates roughly as tapir guava, a name that reflects both its guava-like fruit and the animals that eat it.

The flowers are also large by melastome standards. They are often white to pink, with spreading petals and a cluster of stamens. Like most of the family, they have anthers that open by pores and are visited by pollen-collecting bees.

A pioneer in disturbed forest

Bellucia species grow quickly and are common in light gaps, along river margins and in regrowth after clearing. This pioneer strategy, producing a lot of seeds that colonise open ground quickly, is shared with many other melastome trees such as Miconia. In regenerating forest, pioneers such as these help restore shade and provide food for animals before slower-growing species return.

Fruit and wildlife

Large fruits attract larger animals, including monkeys and other mammals, as well as birds, and these animals disperse the seeds. In this way a Bellucia tree plays a role as a food source in gaps and clearings. For more on how melastome fruit types shape dispersal, see seed dispersal in melastomes.

Human uses

The fruits are eaten locally in some regions, either fresh or used in drinks and preserves, and trees are sometimes left standing when land is cleared. As with any wild fruit, correct identification matters. Do not eat any plant unless you are certain of what it is and that it is safe. More on food uses is in edible melastomes and the black mouth.

Cultivation

Bellucia is a tree of hot, humid lowlands and is rarely cultivated outside tropical botanic gardens. It needs warmth all year, high humidity and plenty of space.

Key takeaways

  • A genus of fast-growing trees from tropical American forest gaps.
  • Large flowers and large, edible fruits are distinctive within the family.
  • A useful example of a pioneer tree that feeds wildlife while forest recovers.
