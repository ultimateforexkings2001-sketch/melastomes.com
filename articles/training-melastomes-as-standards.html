Training a Melastome as a Standard or Bonsai-Style Tree

OC
Owen Castillo, Plant Ecology Writer
•July 10, 2026•6 min read

Left alone, many shrub-forming melastomes — Tibouchina in particular — tend toward a loose, somewhat leggy growth habit. With some deliberate pruning over time, several of these species respond well to being trained into a "standard" form: a single clear trunk topped with a rounded canopy, similar in shape to a small ornamental tree, or even a more deliberate bonsai-style planting for smaller specimens.

Why this works on melastomes specifically

Species that bloom on new growth — again, Tibouchina is the clearest example — tolerate repeated pruning well, since each round of cutting back encourages fresh branching and new flowering wood rather than permanently reducing bloom potential. That tolerance for cutting is exactly what makes standard or bonsai-style training realistic, as opposed to species that bloom only on old wood and resent heavy pruning.

The basic training process

  1. Select a young plant with a reasonably straight main stem, and stake it if needed to encourage vertical growth.
  2. Remove side shoots along the lower portion of the stem as it grows, leaving growth concentrated at the top.
  3. Once the plant reaches the desired trunk height, pinch out the growing tip to encourage branching at the top, forming the beginnings of a canopy.
  4. Continue shaping the canopy with light, regular pruning rather than infrequent heavy cuts.
A standard-trained Tibouchina takes patience over a full growing season or more — there's no shortcut to the trunk actually thickening and lignifying.

Ongoing care

Trained specimens need slightly more attentive watering and feeding than a sprawling shrub of the same species, since a smaller root-to-canopy ratio is being asked to support a more concentrated top growth. Otherwise, the same general care covered in our beginner's guide applies.
