Centradenia: A Trailing Melastome for Hanging Baskets

OC
Owen Castillo, Plant Ecology Writer
•July 9, 2026•5 min read

Most melastomes grow upright — shrubs, small trees, or low groundcovers. Centradenia does something a little different: a small genus of roughly a dozen species native to Mexico and Central America, several of which have a distinctly trailing, cascading growth habit that has made them a modest but genuine presence in hanging-basket cultivation.

What sets it apart

Centradenia inaequilateralis, the most commonly cultivated species, produces slender, arching stems lined with small leaves and clusters of pink-purple flowers. Its cultivar 'Cascade' has been recognized with the Royal Horticultural Society's Award of Garden Merit — a distinction given to plants judged to perform reliably well in real garden conditions, not just in ideal circumstances.

Growing Centradenia

Its trailing habit makes it well suited to elevated pots, hanging baskets, or spilling over a raised bed edge, rather than the upright container growing typical of Tibouchina or Monochaetum. Light and moisture requirements otherwise follow the family's general pattern — bright but filtered light, consistent moisture, and protection from hard frost.

A genus doesn't need to be large or famous to fill a genuinely useful niche — Centradenia's whole appeal is doing one specific thing, trailing growth, better than most of its upright relatives.
